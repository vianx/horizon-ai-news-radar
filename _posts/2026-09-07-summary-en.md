German startup Isar Aerospace successfully launched its two-stage Spectrum rocket into low Earth orbit from Andøya Spaceport in Norway on Saturday, becoming the first privately developed rocket to reach orbit from continental Europe. The 28-meter rocket lifted off at 22:12 local time and deployed five small satellites and an experimental payload. This milestone marks a significant step for European space autonomy and commercial space capabilities, demonstrating that private European companies can achieve orbital launches independently. It could boost the region's competitiveness in the global small satellite launch market and reduce reliance on non-European launch providers. The Spectrum rocket is designed for small and medium-sized payloads, and this launch served as both its qualification mission and first payload-carrying flight. The launch was licensed by the Norwegian Civil Aviation Authority, and Andøya Spaceport, which opened in 2023, is the first orbital launch site in continental Europe.

## [Why Rewriting Software from Scratch Often Fails](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/6/theres-no-limit-to-how-bad-code-can-get/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Simon Willison shared a comment on Lobste.rs arguing that rewriting legacy software from scratch rarely succeeds, based on his experience. He suggests that the old system remains a moving target and the new team lacks context, leading to two systems in production and continued technical debt. This insight is significant for software engineers and project managers facing overwhelming technical debt, as it challenges the common 'big rewrite' approach. It highlights the importance of incremental migration and testing over risky greenfield projects, which can save companies time and resources. Willison references Will Larson's article 'Migrations: the sole scalable fix to tech debt' as a responsible approach. He recommends shoring up the old system with automated testing and targeted refactors instead of a full rewrite.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 6, 09:08

**Background**: Technical debt refers to the implied cost of additional rework caused by choosing an easy solution now instead of a better approach that would take longer. A 'big rewrite' is when a team decides to replace a legacy system entirely, often due to accumulated technical debt. However, such rewrites frequently fail because the old system continues to evolve and the new system may not fully replicate its behavior.
