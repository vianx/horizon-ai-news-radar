## [Cloudflare Runs Kimi and GLM with KV Cache Quantization](https://blog.cloudflare.com/smaller-faster-safer-models/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Cloudflare published a blog post detailing how it serves Kimi and GLM models at scale using techniques like KV cache quantization to make them smaller, faster, and safer. The post highlights their transparency about using FP8 KV cache quantization, which reduces memory footprint and improves throughput. This is significant because it demonstrates a major cloud provider's approach to optimizing open-weight models for production, which can influence how other providers deploy AI models. The transparency about KV cache quantization is valuable for developers who rely on these endpoints, as it affects model quality and performance. The post specifically mentions running Kimi K2.6 and GLM models, and the community discussion notes that only Kimi K2.6 was tested for KV cache sensitivity. Cloudflare uses FP8 KV cache quantization, which can degrade quality more than weight quantization, but they claim it maintains acceptable performance.

hackernews · ascorbic · Aug 3, 17:08 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49158581)

**Background**: KV cache quantization is a technique to reduce the memory footprint of the key-value cache used during inference, enabling longer context windows and higher throughput. It is a common optimization in serving large language models, but it can introduce accuracy loss, especially at lower bit widths like INT4. Cloudflare's approach is notable for its transparency about this trade-off.

Niklas Gruhn coined the term 'meat proxy' to describe people who blindly relay AI-generated output without reading or understanding it. He urges users to read, understand, and validate AI output before responding in their own words. This term highlights a common misuse of AI in communication, where people act as mere conduits for AI output, potentially spreading misinformation or low-quality content. It encourages critical thinking and adds value to human-AI collaboration, relevant to current debates on AI usage in professional and personal settings. Gruhn notes that reading AI output is extra effort because it is verbose, contains plausible nonsense, and is jargon-dense, citing an example from Claude. He emphasizes that relaying AI output without adding value is not helpful, as others can interact with AI directly.

The concept is part of broader discussions about AI misuse and the importance of human oversight in AI-assisted workflows.
